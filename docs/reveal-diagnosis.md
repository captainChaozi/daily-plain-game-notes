# Daily Plain Game: diagnose each reveal with one cause

Source: [How to get better at estimation games](https://plaingame.fun/blog/how-to-improve-estimation) · Play: [Daily Plain Game](https://plaingame.fun/)

After a round, looking only at your altitude teaches little. Name one cause instead.

| What happened | What to try next |
|---|---|
| Off by a factor of ten or more | Check the unit and order of magnitude first |
| A plausible object, but its value surprised you | Keep it as a new reference point, after checking its scope |
| Your answer was not recognised | Use the full name or pick a suggestion from the collection |
| You knew a good answer but ran out of time | Practise spotting the category and input type sooner |
| Points looked good but altitude was lower | That is timed drift, not a scoring error |

## Why the last row happens

In timed rounds each accuracy point is worth ten metres. After three free seconds the plane loses four metres per second, up to 108 metres over 30 seconds. Your points are not reduced; only the final height is.

## Breaking a number into parts

When you have a sensible model, estimate pieces and combine them, for example 12 rows of about 8 seats is roughly 96 seats. When you do not, use a known reference instead. Plain Game is mostly factual estimation, so decomposition will not solve every question.
